## Interpretation and precedence

1. Applicable law, regulator requirements and binding licence conditions take precedence.
2. Formally approved institutional statutes and academic regulations take precedence over this framework where they are more specific.
3. This framework governs academic governance and internal quality assurance.
4. `ACADEMIC_CURRICULUM_ARCHITECTURE.md` governs academic structure, programme design, outcomes, progression and academic model.
5. `ACADEMIC_CONTENT_DEVELOPMENT_STANDARD.md` governs academic content and source development.
6. `UNIVERSITY_MASTER_BLUEPRINT.md` governs the intended product and technical architecture.
7. Approved policies, procedures and Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) implement these standards.

A conflict must be recorded and escalated. No employee, developer, founder or committee may silently resolve a material policy conflict by changing software or operational practice.

# 1. Purpose, Scope and Governance Commitments

## 1.1 Purpose

This framework establishes how the Audio University makes, records, reviews and assures academic decisions. It defines:

- The relationship between institutional governance, executive management and academic authority.
- The authority and responsibilities of the Governing Board, Academic Council, committees, colleges, departments and programme teams.
- Decision rights and separation of academic, commercial and technical authority.
- Appointment, qualification, workload and performance governance for academic staff.
- Approval and change control for programmes, courses, learning resources and assessments.
- Internal and external review, assessment moderation and academic standards assurance.
- Student voice, complaints, appeals, support and protection.
- Risk management, evidence management, audit trails and continuous improvement.
- Governance information and workflows that the digital platform must support.

The framework is designed to make academic quality demonstrable. It is not enough for the institution to state that it follows high standards; it must retain evidence that decisions were made by authorised people, according to defined criteria, and that identified problems were addressed.

## 1.2 Scope

This framework applies to:

- Institutional academic strategy and academic regulations.
- Establishment, suspension, material change and closure of academic units.
- Qualification and programme proposals.
- Programme and course specifications and versions.
- Learning outcomes and curriculum mapping.
- Lesson and learning-resource approval.
- Assessment design, marking, moderation, reassessment and appeals.
- Academic staff appointment and external academic engagement.
- Student admission, progression, recognition of prior learning and academic records.
- Online, distance, audio-first, blended and other approved delivery modes.
- Academic quality monitoring, periodic review and external review.
- Academic data, records, evidence and public academic claims.

It applies to credit-bearing and non-credit learning, while recognising that their legal and academic requirements differ.

## 1.3 Institutional commitments

The University commits to:

1. **Academic independence:** Academic standards cannot be lowered to meet sales targets, launch dates or software limitations.
2. **Lawful operation:** No regulated award or protected institutional claim may be made without the required authority.
3. **Student-centred fairness:** Rules are published, consistently applied and subject to fair review and appeal.
4. **Evidence-based decisions:** Consequential decisions must be supported by recorded evidence.
5. **Qualified judgement:** Academic decisions are made by people with appropriate expertise and delegated authority.
6. **Independent scrutiny:** Material academic proposals and high-stakes assessments receive review independent of their authors.
7. **Transparency:** Students receive accurate information about status, outcomes, assessment, costs, progression and limitations.
8. **Proportionality:** Controls are proportionate to academic risk, student impact and institutional scale.
9. **Accessibility and inclusion:** Governance and quality review consider diverse learner needs and accessible participation.
10. **Continuous improvement:** Findings lead to owned actions, deadlines and verification of effectiveness.
11. **Privacy and confidentiality:** Personal, assessment and staff information is accessed only for legitimate purposes.
12. **Audio-first integrity:** Audio is the primary learning medium where pedagogically appropriate, but listening activity is not treated as proof of competence.

## 1.4 What this framework does not claim

This framework does not by itself:

- Establish the University as a legally recognised higher education institution.
- Authorise use of the word “University” in any jurisdiction.
- Authorise the award of degrees or formal academic credit.
- Guarantee acceptance of awards by another institution, employer, regulator or country.
- Constitute external accreditation.
- Replace local legal advice, regulator requirements or professional accreditation criteria.
- Require the institution to establish every committee before it has the scale or legal status to do so.

The institution must accurately disclose its current legal status and the scope of any authorisation it has obtained.

# 2. Governance Architecture and Institutional Separation

## 2.1 Three distinct governance domains

The institution shall distinguish three domains that must cooperate but must not improperly control one another.

| Domain | Primary responsibility | Examples | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional governance | Purpose, legal compliance, fiduciary oversight, strategy, risk and sustainability | Governing Board, audit/risk oversight | Does not directly change individual academic marks or bypass academic due process |
| Academic governance | Academic standards, programmes, assessment, awards, academic integrity and quality | Academic Council and academic committees | Does not disregard law, approved budgets or institutional risk controls |
| Executive and operational management | Delivery of approved strategy, staffing, finance, technology, marketing and services | CEO/President/Executive, Operations, Finance, Technology | Cannot unilaterally approve academic standards or alter academic outcomes |

Commercial success and academic quality are related but not interchangeable. Financial performance may inform whether a programme is sustainable; it cannot justify approving an academically deficient programme.

## 2.2 Governance structure

The following is the target governance model. The final legal form and committee structure must be adapted to the selected jurisdiction and institutional scale.

```text
Governing Board
|
|-- Board Audit, Risk & Compliance Oversight
|
|-- University Executive
|   |-- Finance & Operations
|   |-- Technology & Digital Services
|   |-- Student Services
|   |-- Marketing & Partnerships
|   |-- People / Human Resources
|
|-- University Academic Council
    |-- Academic Quality & Standards Committee
    |-- Programme Approval & Review Committee
    |-- Assessment & Academic Integrity Committee
    |-- Research Ethics Committee (when research activity requires it)
    |-- College Boards
        |-- Department / Discipline Boards
            |-- Programme Committees
                |-- Course Coordinators and Teaching Teams
```

The Academic Council must have a direct reporting route to the Governing Board for material academic risks. The executive may provide resources and operational support but must not suppress or rewrite academic findings.

## 2.3 Lean-start principle

The University should not create a large bureaucracy before it has programmes, students and sufficient staff. It must, however, preserve the functions.

At an early stage, one properly qualified person may hold more than one operational role if:

- The combination is permitted by law.
- Competence and workload are documented.
- Conflicts of interest are declared.
- Consequential decisions receive independent review.
- The person does not become the sole author, reviewer and approver of the same high-risk decision.
- A clear escalation route exists.

A committee function may temporarily be fulfilled by a formally appointed panel. It must not be represented as a constituted statutory committee until it has actually been constituted.

## 2.4 Governing Board

### Core mandate

The Governing Board is responsible for institutional stewardship, lawful operation, financial sustainability and oversight of institutional risk.

### Responsibilities

- Approve institutional mission, strategy and risk appetite.
- Ensure the institution has a lawful establishment and operating route.
- Approve annual budget and material financial commitments.
- Oversee executive performance and institutional controls.
- Ensure sufficient resources for academic quality and student protection.
- Approve institutional statutes and governance framework, subject to law.
- Appoint or confirm the senior executive and, where appropriate, the senior academic leader.
- Receive reports from the Academic Council and quality function.
- Review material regulatory, reputational, safeguarding, data and continuity risks.
- Approve institutional merger, closure or material restructuring, subject to legal and student-protection requirements.
- Ensure that public claims about legal status, recognition and accreditation are accurate.

### Reserved matters

The Board may reserve decisions concerning institutional strategy, budget, legal structure, major risk exposure and appointment of senior officers. It must not use a reserved matter to bypass academic review of curriculum, assessment or an individual student's result.

### Board academic oversight

The Board shall receive at least an annual academic assurance report covering:

- Programme portfolio and approval status.
- Academic quality findings.
- Student progression and completion.
- Assessment integrity and moderation.
- External reviewer findings.
- Academic staffing and resource adequacy.
- Student complaints and appeals trends.
- Material academic risks and overdue actions.
- Regulatory and accreditation readiness.

The Board may require an improvement plan or resource response. Academic judgements remain with the authorised academic bodies.

## 2.5 University Executive

The Executive implements approved institutional strategy and ensures that the University has the people, systems, finance and services needed to operate.

It is responsible for:

- Operational planning and delivery.
- Financial control and procurement.
- Technology, cybersecurity and service availability.
- Student administration and support operations.
- Marketing and public communications.
- Human resources administration.
- Business continuity and incident management.
- Providing accurate operational data to academic bodies.
- Implementing approved academic decisions.

The Executive must not:

- Change programme learning outcomes without academic approval.
- Alter grades or assessment outcomes outside approved procedures.
- Publish an unapproved programme as approved.
- Override an academic integrity finding through commercial pressure.
- Use student engagement metrics as a substitute for academic achievement.
- Change academic regulations directly in production.

## 2.6 University Academic Council

The Academic Council is the senior academic authority of the institution, subject to applicable law and institutional statutes.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain academic standards and academic regulations.
- Approve academic policies within its delegated authority.
- Approve, conditionally approve, suspend or recommend closure of programmes.
- Approve qualification and programme learning outcomes.
- Ensure curriculum coherence and assessment validity.
- Oversee academic integrity and assessment standards.
- Review institutional academic quality reports.
- Consider external academic review findings.
- Oversee academic appeals framework and delegated appeal panels.
- Recommend awards or confer awards only where lawful authority has been established.
- Ensure student interests are considered in academic decisions.
- Report material academic risks to the Governing Board.

### Membership principles

Membership should include:

- Senior academic leader / Chair.
- Academic representatives from active colleges or disciplines.
- Academic Quality & Standards representation.
- Programme leadership representation.
- A student representative, with appropriate confidentiality safeguards.
- External academic member(s), where feasible and appropriate.
- Secretary with responsibility for governance records.

The exact composition, term lengths, quorum and voting rules must be set in approved Terms of Reference (ToR).

### Independence

A member must declare an interest and withdraw from discussion or decision where impartiality may reasonably be questioned. The Chair must ensure that commercial, personal or authorship interests do not dominate academic decisions.

# 3. Academic Committees: Terms of Reference

Each committee must have a written ToR defining purpose, authority, membership, quorum, meeting frequency, reporting, records, conflicts and delegated limits.

## 3.1 Academic Quality & Standards Committee (AQSC)

**Purpose:** Maintain and improve the effectiveness of institutional academic quality assurance.

**Core responsibilities:**
- Maintain the internal quality assurance framework.
- Review annual programme monitoring reports.
- Monitor programme and course review schedules.
- Review PLO/CLO achievement evidence and curriculum mapping gaps.
- Monitor assessment moderation and external review completion.
- Review student feedback, complaints and appeals trends.
- Monitor academic staffing and learning-resource adequacy.
- Review accessibility, inclusion and student-support evidence.
- Track corrective and enhancement actions.
- Recommend institutional improvements to Academic Council.
- Prepare the annual academic assurance report.

**Authority:** May require programme teams to provide evidence and action plans; may recommend conditions, suspension or further review. It may not unilaterally change grades or approve a new qualification unless explicitly delegated.

**Meeting frequency:** At least once each academic term during active delivery, plus an annual consolidated review.

**Outputs:** Minutes, annual quality report, action register, risk escalations and recommendations to Academic Council.

## 3.2 Programme Approval & Review Committee (PARC)

**Purpose:** Evaluate new programmes and material programme changes before Academic Council approval.

**Responsibilities:**
- Evaluate programme rationale and demand evidence.
- Verify alignment with institutional mission and portfolio strategy.
- Review graduate profile, PLOs and qualification level proposal.
- Review curriculum structure, sequencing and workload.
- Verify direct assessment of every PLO.
- Evaluate staffing, resources, library and student support.
- Assess suitability of online/audio-first delivery.
- Confirm external reference points and independent review.
- Review legal and regulatory feasibility evidence.
- Recommend approval, conditional approval, revision or rejection.
- Review material programme changes and closure proposals.

**Independence:** The proposal author or programme sponsor may present evidence but must not chair the committee's decision on their own proposal.

**Outputs:** Formal recommendation, conditions, evidence checklist and decision record.

## 3.3 Assessment & Academic Integrity Committee (AAIC)

**Purpose:** Protect the validity, fairness, reliability and security of assessment and academic achievement.

**Responsibilities:**
- Maintain assessment and grading standards.
- Review assessment blueprints and moderation policy.
- Monitor grade distributions, pass/fail patterns and reassessment.
- Oversee academic misconduct procedures and consistency of sanctions.
- Review identity/authorship assurance arrangements.
- Monitor responsible AI rules in assessment.
- Review assessment appeals trends.
- Ensure appropriate reasonable adjustments.
- Review high-stakes assessment incidents and compromised assessment material.
- Recommend improvements to Academic Council.

**Boundaries:** Individual cases may be delegated to trained panels. Committee members with conflicts must recuse themselves. The committee must not presume misconduct solely from an automated AI-detection score.

## 3.4 Research Ethics Committee (REC)

The REC must be established before the institution undertakes research requiring ethics review, including relevant research involving human participants, personal data, sensitive information or animals.

**Responsibilities:**
- Review research protocols within its approved scope.
- Assess consent, risk, privacy, safeguarding and participant protection.
- Review conflicts of interest and data management.
- Monitor adverse events and protocol deviations.
- Maintain decisions and conditions.
- Refer matters outside its competence to the appropriate authority.

The University must not claim to have a functioning research ethics system until the committee, membership, expertise, procedures and records are in place.

## 3.5 College Board

**Purpose:** Coordinate academic standards and delivery across a college.

**Responsibilities:**
- Review programme delivery and academic performance.
- Review staffing and resource sufficiency.
- Monitor curriculum coherence across departments.
- Review programme committee reports.
- Coordinate discipline-specific external engagement.
- Escalate academic risks to Academic Council and AQSC.

## 3.6 Department / Discipline Board

**Purpose:** Maintain disciplinary coherence and subject standards.

**Responsibilities:**
- Review course specifications and subject currency.
- Ensure appropriate subject expertise in teaching and assessment.
- Review prerequisite relationships and curriculum dependencies.
- Monitor course-level outcomes and academic standards.
- Recommend course changes.
- Coordinate peer review and academic development.

## 3.7 Programme Committee

**Purpose:** Monitor the quality and effectiveness of a specific programme.

**Responsibilities:**
- Review programme delivery against the approved specification.
- Monitor student progression and outcomes.
- Review course coordination and assessment schedules.
- Monitor PLO achievement and curriculum mapping.
- Review student feedback and academic support cases in aggregate.
- Track programme-level risks and actions.
- Prepare annual programme monitoring reports.
- Recommend programme improvements.

## 3.8 Committee operating rules

Every committee must maintain:

- Approved ToR and version.
- Named Chair and Secretary.
- Membership and appointment dates.
- Quorum and voting rules.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations.
- Agenda and papers.
- Attendance record.
- Minutes recording decisions and rationale.
- Action log with owner and due date.
- Escalation route.
- Annual effectiveness review.

Minutes must distinguish discussion, recommendation, decision and action. A meeting discussion is not an approval unless a valid decision was made under the committee's authority and quorum.

# 4. Decision Rights and Delegation

## 4.1 Decision-rights principles

1. Authority must be explicit, not inferred from job title or system permissions.
2. Delegation must be documented and time-bounded where appropriate.
3. Delegated authority cannot exceed the delegator's own authority.
4. Legal or regulator-reserved decisions cannot be delegated internally where prohibited.
5. High-impact academic decisions require a recorded rationale and evidence.
6. Software permissions must reflect approved decision rights; they do not create those rights.
7. A user who enters data is not automatically authorised to approve it.
8. Emergency actions must be limited, logged and reviewed after the event.

## 4.2 Decision categories

| Decision category | Primary recommendation / preparation | Academic decision authority | Institutional / external dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional mission and strategy | Executive and Board committees | Academic Council advises on academic implications | Governing Board |
| Academic regulations | Academic Affairs / AQSC | Academic Council | Board or regulator where required |
| New programme proposal | Programme sponsor / department | PARC recommends; Academic Council approves | Legal/regulator approval where required |
| Programme material change | Programme Committee / Department | PARC and Academic Council according to impact | Regulator/accreditor where required |
| Course specification | Course Coordinator / Department | Delegated academic approval route | Programme rules |
| Lesson content approval | Author / subject reviewer | Designated academic content approver | Copyright/licensing requirements |
| Assessment design | Course team | Academic approval and moderation | Professional/regulatory rules where applicable |
| Individual marking | Qualified marker | Authorised marker under approved rubric | Moderation and appeal rules |
| Grade moderation | Moderator / external reviewer | Designated academic board or authorised panel | External requirements |
| Individual grade appeal | Independent appeal panel | Appeal authority defined in regulations | External ombuds/regulator if applicable |
| Academic misconduct case | Trained investigator / case officer | Independent authorised panel | Law and due process |
| Faculty appointment | Academic lead / HR | Academic appointment panel | Employment law and regulator |
| Budget and procurement | Executive / Finance | Academic input on academic need | Governing Board thresholds |
| Technology architecture | Technology lead | Academic requirements consulted | Executive / Board risk controls |
| Public accreditation claim | Compliance / Communications | Academic status verification | Legal approval and official external decision |
| Award recommendation / conferral | Registry / Programme Board | Academic Council or delegated award board | Must be legally authorised |

## 4.3 RACI matrix

R = Responsible for doing the work; A = Accountable for the decision; C = Consulted; I = Informed. The matrix is a default model and must be adapted to the final statutes and law.

| Activity | Board | Executive | Academic Council | AQSC | PARC | Programme / Department | Quality Office |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional strategy | A | R | C | I | I | I | C |
| Academic regulations | I | C | A | R | C | C | R |
| New programme design | I | C | A | C | R | R | C |
| Programme approval review | I | C | A | C | R | C | R |
| Annual programme monitoring | I | I | I | A | C | R | R |
| Course specification | I | C | I | C | C | A/R* | C |
| Lesson content review | I | I | I | C | I | A/R* | C |
| Assessment moderation policy | I | C | A | C | I | R | R |
| Academic integrity policy | I | C | A | C | I | C | R |
| Academic risk escalation | A | R | R | R | C | R | R |
| Institutional quality report | I | C | A | R | C | C | R |
| Academic staffing standards | I | C | A | C | I | R | C |
| Academic record controls | I | R | A | C | I | C | C |

\*The accountable role must be a formally delegated academic authority, not simply the person who created the content.

## 4.4 Reserved and prohibited decisions

The following actions are prohibited unless expressly authorised by law and approved institutional regulations:

- A commercial employee changing a grade.
- A developer changing a student's academic status directly in the database.
- A programme author approving their own high-stakes programme without independent review.
- A marker acting as the sole appeal decision-maker for their own disputed mark.
- Marketing staff describing a programme as accredited without verified evidence and approval.
- A platform administrator silently changing credit, grading or progression rules.
- An executive instructing academic staff to pass or fail a student for commercial reasons.
- A committee making decisions without quorum and later recording them as valid.
- Deleting or overwriting a consequential academic decision without an auditable correction process.

# 5. Academic Leadership, Staff Appointment and Faculty Governance

## 5.1 Academic leadership roles

The institution should plan for the following functions as it grows:

- Senior academic leader / Provost-equivalent.
- Dean or College Academic Lead.
- Department / Discipline Head.
- Programme Director.
- Course Coordinator.
- Subject Matter Expert.
- Academic Reviewer.
- Assessment Marker.
- Assessment Moderator.
- External Examiner / External Reviewer, where appropriate.
- Academic Quality Officer.
- Academic Registry / Records Officer.
- Academic Adviser.
- Learning Designer and Accessibility Specialist.
- Librarian / Information Literacy Specialist.

Role titles must not imply qualifications, authority or statutory standing that the institution does not possess.

## 5.2 Appointment principles

Academic appointments must be based on:

- Verified academic qualifications relevant to the role.
- Subject knowledge and current disciplinary competence.
- Teaching, assessment or professional experience appropriate to the role.
- Ability to design or evaluate learning outcomes and assessment.
- Understanding of academic integrity and student fairness.
- Digital and online teaching competence where required.
- Communication and accessibility awareness.
- Professional standing and references where appropriate.
- Conflict-of-interest and conduct checks proportionate to role and law.

The University must not appoint an individual as an academic reviewer solely because they are famous, have a large online audience, or are commercially valuable.

## 5.3 Qualification and experience verification

Before appointment or assignment to a consequential academic role, retain:

- Verified identity and contact details.
- Qualification title, awarding institution and award date.
- Verification evidence or documented verification attempt.
- Relevant professional registration where required.
- Curriculum vitae and subject expertise record.
- Teaching and assessment experience.
- Research or professional portfolio where relevant.
- References and due diligence.
- Appointment decision and role scope.
- Workload and contract/engagement terms.
- Conflict-of-interest declaration.
- Required training and induction.
- Review and development records.

If a qualification cannot be independently verified, record the limitation and do not represent it as verified.

## 5.4 Role-specific expectations

### Senior academic leader

Must demonstrate appropriate senior academic experience, academic leadership, quality assurance competence and the ability to protect academic independence.

### Programme Director

Must have subject and programme-level competence sufficient to oversee PLOs, curriculum coherence, staffing, assessment and annual monitoring.

### Course Coordinator

Must be competent in the course discipline and responsible for course specification, delivery coordination, assessment integrity and course review.

### Subject Matter Expert

Must demonstrate relevant subject expertise. An SME may contribute to content without automatically holding authority to approve a programme or confer an award.

### Academic Reviewer

Must be sufficiently independent of the material being reviewed and qualified for the subject and level under review.

### External Reviewer

Must disclose institutional, financial, personal and professional relationships that could affect impartiality. The appointment record must explain why their expertise is appropriate.

## 5.5 Academic workload and capacity

The University must record and review academic workload, including:

- Teaching and learner interaction.
- Assessment design and marking.
- Moderation and external review.
- Student advising.
- Curriculum and content development.
- Academic governance and committee duties.
- Research or professional activity where relevant.
- Continuing professional development.

No individual should be assigned a workload that makes timely feedback, valid assessment or meaningful review unrealistic.

## 5.6 Induction and continuing professional development

Before performing relevant duties, academic staff should receive induction in:

- Academic governance and delegated authority.
- Programme and course standards.
- Assessment and moderation.
- Academic integrity and responsible AI.
- Student complaints and appeals.
- Accessibility and inclusive learning.
- Audio-first learning design.
- Source verification and intellectual property.
- Privacy, confidentiality and secure systems.
- Use of the academic platform and records.

Professional development needs must be reviewed at least annually.

## 5.7 Performance and conduct

Academic performance review should consider:

- Quality of teaching and learning design.
- Timeliness and quality of feedback.
- Assessment reliability and fairness.
- Student outcomes interpreted in context.
- Contribution to programme improvement.
- Peer and external review.
- Professional development.
- Compliance with academic integrity and governance duties.

Student satisfaction scores alone must not determine academic performance. Nor should high pass rates be treated as proof of excellent teaching without examining standards and student work.

# 6. Programme, Course and Content Approval Governance

## 6.1 Programme lifecycle

Every programme must move through controlled states:

```text
Concept
  ↓
Proposal Draft
  ↓
Internal Academic Design
  ↓
Independent Review
  ↓
Quality and Regulatory Review
  ↓
PARC Recommendation
  ↓
Academic Council Decision
  ↓
Regulatory Approval (if required)
  ↓
Approved for Delivery
  ↓
Active Delivery
  ↓
Annual Monitoring
  ↓
Periodic Review
  ↓
Continue / Amend / Suspend / Close
```

The system must distinguish internal academic approval from external legal or regulatory authorisation. A programme cannot be marked “Approved for Delivery” until all mandatory approval conditions have been met.

## 6.2 Programme proposal evidence

The proposal must include, at minimum:

- Strategic and academic rationale.
- Target learner and evidence of need.
- Graduate profile and programme educational aims.
- Measurable PLOs.
- Proposed qualification and level mapping.
- Curriculum structure and course sequence.
- PLO-to-course and PLO-to-assessment maps.
- Workload and credit proposal, where applicable.
- Admission, progression and completion rules.
- Assessment strategy and moderation plan.
- Staffing and external expertise plan.
- Library, learning resources and rights evidence.
- Online/audio-first suitability assessment.
- Accessibility and student support plan.
- Financial and operational sustainability.
- Legal and regulatory feasibility.
- Risk assessment.
- Independent external academic review.
- Decision record and conditions.

The programme approval criteria and gates in `ACADEMIC_CURRICULUM_ARCHITECTURE.md` remain mandatory companion requirements.

## 6.3 Course approval

A course must not be published as an approved academic course until it has:

- Stable code and version.
- Rationale and programme position.
- Level and workload specification.
- CLOs mapped to relevant PLOs.
- Prerequisite and co-requisite rules.
- Unit and lesson map.
- Teaching and learning activities.
- Assessment blueprint and rubrics.
- Source and reading register.
- Rights and licensing checks.
- Delivery, accessibility and support plan.
- Named course owner and reviewer.
- Approval record and effective date.

## 6.4 Lesson and learning-resource approval

Lesson-level production and source governance are specified in `ACADEMIC_CONTENT_DEVELOPMENT_STANDARD.md`.

The governance framework adds the following controls:

- The author and reviewer must be identifiable.
- Scientific, factual, mathematical, legal and other high-impact claims must be traceable to suitable sources.
- Rights and licensing status must be recorded before publication.
- AI-assisted material must receive qualified human review.
- A lesson must be checked for alignment with its approved learning objectives.
- Audio quality, pronunciation, transcript and accessibility must be reviewed.
- Material changes must trigger review of affected assessments and dependent lessons.
- A published lesson must retain its version and approval history.

## 6.5 Content states and approval controls

The standard content lifecycle is:

`DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → APPROVED → PUBLISHED → ARCHIVED`

Rules:

- `APPROVED` requires a valid academic approval record.
- `PUBLISHED` requires both academic approval and operational publication permission.
- `ARCHIVED` preserves historical access and audit records as required.
- Rejected or withdrawn content must not be silently deleted.
- A material revision creates a new version.
- The platform must prevent an author from approving their own material where independent approval is required.

## 6.6 Programme and course change classification

| Change | Classification | Minimum governance route |
|---|---|---|
| Typographical correction with no academic meaning change | Non-substantive | Delegated owner and change log |
| Audio re-recording preserving approved meaning | Limited | Course owner and content QA |
| New example or explanation with no outcome/assessment impact | Limited | Subject review under delegated policy |
| Change to lesson learning objective | Academic | Course/department academic approval |
| Change to CLO or assessment | Material academic | Programme review and authorised approval |
| Change to prerequisites, level or workload | Material academic | Programme Committee and formal approval |
| Change to required course structure or PLOs | Major | PARC and Academic Council |
| Change to award title, level, credit or duration | Major / regulatory | Academic Council and regulator where required |
| Suspension or closure | Major / student-protection | Academic Council, Board and regulator where required |

A change-impact analysis must identify affected students, versions, outcomes, assessments, prerequisites, transcripts, public information, regulatory conditions and teach-out obligations.

# 7. Assessment Governance and Academic Standards

## 7.1 Assessment governance principles

Assessment must be valid, reliable, fair, transparent, authentic, secure, accessible and proportionate to the learning outcomes and qualification level.

Assessment decisions must be based on student evidence against published criteria. Commercial status, subscription tier, marketing value, listening time or personal relationships must not influence academic grades.

## 7.2 Assessment design and approval

Each assessment must have:

- Stable assessment ID and version.
- Purpose and assessment type.
- CLO and PLO mapping.
- Weighting and timing.
- Individual/group and open/closed-book conditions.
- Instructions and permitted resources.
- Rubric or marking scheme.
- Pass and grade rules where applicable.
- Attempt and reassessment rules.
- Feedback arrangements.
- Identity and authorship controls.
- Accessibility and reasonable adjustment provisions.
- Security classification and exposure controls.
- Internal moderation record.
- External review where required.

The assessment author must not be the sole approver of a high-stakes assessment.

## 7.3 Marking standards

Markers must:

- Be appropriately competent in the subject and assessment type.
- Use the approved rubric and current assessment version.
- Declare conflicts of interest.
- Record sufficient rationale for consequential judgements.
- Provide feedback within the published service standard.
- Protect student confidentiality.
- Refer suspected misconduct through the approved process rather than applying informal penalties.

## 7.4 Internal moderation

Internal moderation must check the validity and consistency of marking and assessment design.

Depending on assessment type and risk, moderation may include:

- Pre-assessment review of task and rubric.
- Review of a sample of marked work.
- Double marking for selected high-stakes work.
- Blind second marking where appropriate.
- Review of borderline and fail cases.
- Review of grade distributions and unusual patterns.
- Calibration exercises among markers.
- Reconciliation of material differences.

Sampling rates and moderation methods must be defined in the Assessment Policy according to risk and volume. This framework does not impose one universal percentage.

## 7.5 External examination and review

For formal qualifications and high-stakes programmes, the institution should establish independent external review arrangements where appropriate and required.

External reviewers may examine:

- Assessment briefs and rubrics.
- Anonymised samples of student work.
- Marking and moderation records.
- Grade distributions.
- PLO achievement evidence.
- Comparability with relevant external standards.
- Responses to prior recommendations.

External reviewers do not replace the institution's own academic responsibility and do not themselves confer accreditation.

## 7.6 Assessment boards and ratification

Where formal awards or progression decisions are made, the University must define an authorised assessment board or delegated panel.

Its ToR must specify:

- Membership and quorum.
- Eligibility and conflict rules.
- Data and evidence required.
- Grade and progression decisions within authority.
- Treatment of missing or disputed marks.
- Compensation, condonement or discretion only where formally permitted.
- Reassessment and repeat decisions.
- Award recommendations.
- Record and appeal requirements.

No board may invent a grading or progression exception during a meeting. Any discretion must be authorised by published regulations and applied consistently.

## 7.7 Audio-first learning and assessment

The University must distinguish:

- Listening activity.
- Learning activity.
- Formative practice.
- Assessment attempt.
- Verified learning outcome achievement.
- Course completion.
- Programme completion.

Listening progress is not, by itself, evidence of mastery. Assessment must measure the intended outcome, not merely recall incidental wording from an audio lesson.

Where outcomes require coding, mathematical reasoning, design, laboratory work, communication, professional practice or research, students must produce suitable performance evidence. The delivery model must support that evidence or the programme must not claim to assess the outcome.

## 7.8 Assessment security and incidents

The institution must define procedures for:

- Suspected question-bank exposure.
- Unauthorised access.
- Identity fraud.
- Platform outage during assessment.
- Lost or corrupted submissions.
- Compromised marking.
- Misconfigured assessment rules.
- Suspected collusion or contract cheating.
- Data breach affecting assessment evidence.

Incident decisions must prioritise fairness to students, preserve evidence and avoid retroactive rule changes without authority.

# 8. Academic Integrity, Misconduct and Responsible AI Governance

## 8.1 Academic integrity principles

The University shall define and consistently address:

- Plagiarism.
- Self-plagiarism where relevant.
- Collusion and unauthorised collaboration.
- Contract cheating.
- Fabrication and falsification.
- Impersonation and identity fraud.
- Unauthorised access to assessment material.
- Misrepresentation of sources or data.
- Copyright infringement.
- Unauthorised AI use or failure to disclose AI use where disclosure is required.
- Research misconduct and ethical breaches.

## 8.2 Fair case process

A misconduct procedure must include:

1. Receipt and recording of allegation.
2. Initial assessment of jurisdiction and evidence.
3. Conflict check and appointment of an impartial case officer.
4. Notice to the student describing the allegation and relevant evidence.
5. Reasonable opportunity to respond.
6. Assessment of evidence using the published standard.
7. Decision by an authorised independent decision-maker or panel.
8. Proportionate outcome under published regulations.
9. Written reasons and appeal route.
10. Secure record retention and trend analysis.

The institution must not impose a penalty solely because an automated detector produced a high score. AI detection may at most be one contextual indicator requiring corroboration.

## 8.3 Responsible AI in teaching and assessment

For each assessment, the course specification must state whether AI use is:

- Prohibited.
- Permitted only for specified purposes.
- Permitted with disclosure.
- Unrestricted within defined academic and ethical boundaries.

The policy must explain permitted tools and uses, disclosure requirements, authorship expectations, data privacy and how concerns are investigated.

The University may use AI to assist content production, translation, accessibility, feedback and administration only under documented human accountability, source verification and privacy controls.

## 8.4 Research integrity

Before research activity begins, separate policies must define research ethics approval, informed consent, data management, authorship, publication, conflicts of interest, misconduct investigation and retention.

# 9. Student Voice, Complaints, Appeals and Protection

## 9.1 Student voice

Students must have meaningful and safe ways to contribute to quality enhancement.

Mechanisms may include:

- Course evaluations.
- Programme surveys.
- Student representation on relevant committees.
- Focus groups and interviews.
- Student-staff liaison meetings.
- Feedback on assessment and support.
- Exit surveys.
- Accessible complaint and suggestion channels.

The institution must publish what feedback led to, while protecting confidentiality. Feedback collection without analysis, action and communication is not an effective student-voice system.

## 9.2 Student representation

Student representatives must:

- Receive clear role descriptions and induction.
- Have access to relevant non-confidential papers.
- Be able to raise concerns without retaliation.
- Declare conflicts where applicable.
- Respect confidential student and staff information.
- Receive feedback on actions taken.

Students must not be exposed to identifiable misconduct, health, disability, financial or disciplinary records unless lawfully required and strictly necessary.

## 9.3 Complaints

A complaints procedure must distinguish:

- Academic judgement disputes.
- Procedural or service complaints.
- Discrimination, harassment or safeguarding concerns.
- Technology and access problems.
- Financial, payment and refund disputes.
- Privacy and data-protection complaints.
- Allegations against staff.

The procedure must state submission channels, acknowledgement, expected response times, escalation, confidentiality, evidence handling and external routes where applicable.

## 9.4 Academic appeals

Appeals must be based on published grounds, which may include:

- Procedural irregularity.
- Relevant evidence that could not reasonably have been presented earlier.
- Bias or conflict of interest.
- A decision inconsistent with published regulations.
- A material error in recording or calculation.

An appeal must not be a simple disagreement with academic judgement unless the applicable rules allow review on that basis.

Appeals must be heard by people independent of the original decision to the extent reasonably possible. The original decision-maker may provide evidence but must not determine the appeal.

## 9.5 Student protection and teach-out

If a programme is suspended, materially changed or closed, the institution must maintain a student-protection plan covering:

- Affected student cohorts.
- Completion options and time limits.
- Equivalent replacement learning.
- Assessment and supervision availability.
- Records and transcript preservation.
- Financial and refund arrangements.
- Communication and support.
- Transfer or partnership options where lawful.
- Complaints and appeal rights.

No student should be silently transferred to a materially different programme version.

## 9.6 Student support quality

Student support must be monitored for:

- Response time.
- Resolution time.
- Accessibility.
- Academic advising quality.
- Technical support effectiveness.
- Early identification of academic difficulty.
- Referral and escalation.
- Student satisfaction and qualitative feedback.

Automated support tools may assist but must not be the only route for urgent, sensitive or consequential student matters.

# 10. Internal Quality Assurance Operating Model

## 10.1 Four levels of quality assurance

| Level | Main question | Accountable function |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional | Is the institution governed, resourced and controlled effectively? | Governing Board and Executive, with Academic Council assurance |
| College / Department | Are academic standards coherent across disciplines and units? | College and Department Boards |
| Programme | Does the programme deliver and assess its approved outcomes? | Programme Committee and Academic Council oversight |
| Course / Unit / Lesson / Assessment | Is each learning component accurate, aligned, accessible and effective? | Course owner, reviewers and delegated academic approver |

## 10.2 Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle

```text
PLAN
Set mission, outcomes, standards, resources, responsibilities and measures
  ↓
DO
Deliver teaching, learning, assessment and student support
  ↓
CHECK
Review student work, outcomes, data, feedback, moderation and external evidence
  ↓
ACT
Approve corrective action, enhancement, owner, deadline and effectiveness check
  ↺
```

The cycle must operate at programme level annually and at institutional level at least annually. Course-level review should occur at a frequency proportionate to risk, change and delivery.

## 10.3 Annual programme monitoring

Each active programme must produce an annual monitoring report containing:

- Student applications, admissions and enrolments.
- Retention, interruption, withdrawal and completion.
- Progression and course performance.
- Grade distributions and reassessment.
- PLO achievement evidence.
- Assessment moderation findings.
- External reviewer feedback.
- Student feedback and representation.
- Complaints and appeals trends.
- Academic integrity cases.
- Staff capacity and changes.
- Learning resources, technology and accessibility.
- Prior-year actions and effectiveness.
- New risks and improvement proposals.

The Programme Committee reviews the report; AQSC evaluates institutional implications; Academic Council receives the report and makes or delegates decisions.

## 10.4 Periodic programme review

Every programme must undergo a deeper review on a defined cycle, normally within a five-year planning horizon or sooner if required by law, regulator, professional body or material risk.

The review must assess:

- Continued relevance and need.
- Curriculum currency and academic depth.
- Qualification-level appropriateness.
- PLO achievement.
- Assessment validity and standards.
- Student progression and completion.
- External comparability.
- Graduate outcomes where reliable data exist.
- Employer and professional feedback.
- Staffing and resource sufficiency.
- Accessibility and student support.
- Delivery-mode effectiveness.
- Risk and sustainability.
- Whether to continue, amend, suspend or close.

## 10.5 Course and lesson review

Course review should consider student performance, CLO achievement, assessment reliability, feedback, sources, accessibility and course changes.

Lesson review should consider factual currency, source integrity, audio quality, learning objective alignment, learner comprehension evidence and rights.

A lesson with outdated or incorrect material must be corrected or withdrawn through version control. The institution must assess whether prior student achievement or assessment is affected.

## 10.6 Corrective and enhancement action

Each action must have:

- Unique action ID.
- Finding or evidence reference.
- Risk/priority rating.
- Required outcome.
- Named accountable owner.
- Due date.
- Resources or dependencies.
- Status.
- Completion evidence.
- Independent effectiveness check where appropriate.
- Closure decision and date.

An action is not closed merely because someone reports that it is complete. The owner must provide evidence and the designated reviewer must verify the result.

# 11. External Review and Accreditation Readiness

## 11.1 Purpose

External review provides independent scrutiny and helps the University test whether its academic standards and quality systems are credible beyond its own internal judgement.

External review is not a substitute for internal governance and does not itself grant legal recognition or accreditation unless the reviewing body is a competent authority acting under a formal process.

## 11.2 External reviewer selection

Reviewers must be selected based on:

- Relevant disciplinary expertise.
- Appropriate academic level and experience.
- Independence from the proposal or assessment under review.
- Absence or management of conflicts of interest.
- Understanding of online/distance provision where relevant.
- Ability to provide evidence-based written findings.

The institution must retain the selection rationale, terms of appointment, scope, declaration of interests and report.

## 11.3 External review scope

Depending on the assignment, reviewers may assess:

- Programme rationale and design.
- Qualification level and outcomes.
- Curriculum and sequencing.
- Assessment design and student work.
- Marking and moderation.
- Academic staffing.
- Learning resources and source quality.
- Audio-first delivery and accessibility.
- Student support.
- Quality monitoring and improvement.
- Comparability with appropriate external reference points.

## 11.4 Response to external findings

Each report must receive a formal institutional response:

- Accept.
- Accept in part, with reasons.
- Do not accept, with evidence-based reasons.

Every accepted recommendation must become a tracked action. Material disagreement must be escalated to Academic Council and recorded.

## 11.5 Accreditation and recognition readiness

The University must maintain a readiness evidence map that connects each relevant external criterion to:

- Policy or process.
- Responsible owner.
- Evidence record.
- Implementation status.
- Gap.
- Corrective action.
- Last verification date.

No application should be submitted until eligibility is confirmed, evidence is complete, material gaps are addressed and the relevant authority or accreditor is verified as competent and appropriate.

The University must not imply that internal approval, external peer review, membership, a partnership or a directory listing is equivalent to accreditation.

# 12. Risk Management, Compliance and Escalation

## 12.1 Academic risk categories

The institutional risk register must include, at minimum:

- Legal and regulatory status.
- Unauthorised award or institutional claims.
- Academic standards and curriculum quality.
- Assessment validity and integrity.
- Faculty qualification and capacity.
- Student protection and programme closure.
- Student complaints and appeals.
- Accessibility and discrimination.
- Research ethics and misconduct.
- Copyright, licensing and source reliability.
- Privacy and academic-record security.
- Cybersecurity and platform availability.
- Cross-border delivery and data transfer.
- Financial sustainability affecting academic delivery.
- External partner and reviewer reliability.
- Reputational harm and misleading marketing.

## 12.2 Risk record

Each risk record must contain:

- Risk ID and title.
- Cause, event and consequence.
- Category.
- Inherent likelihood and impact.
- Existing controls.
- Residual likelihood and impact.
- Risk owner.
- Mitigation and contingency.
- Due date and review date.
- Escalation threshold.
- Evidence and incident links.
- Status and acceptance authority.

Risk scoring methodology must be documented separately and used consistently. Risk ratings must not be manipulated to make unresolved risks appear acceptable.

## 12.3 Escalation triggers

Immediate escalation is required where there is credible evidence of:

- Operation outside legal authorisation.
- False or misleading accreditation claims.
- Systemic assessment compromise.
- Serious academic misconduct or research ethics breach.
- Material student-record alteration or loss.
- Significant privacy or security incident.
- Inability to deliver required teaching or assessment.
- A programme's PLOs cannot be validly assessed.
- Serious harm, discrimination or safeguarding concern.
- Failure to protect students after suspension or closure.

The escalation route must identify the relevant executive, academic authority, Board oversight and external notification obligations.

## 12.4 Whistleblowing and non-retaliation

Staff, students and contractors must have a confidential route to raise good-faith concerns about academic standards, misconduct, fraud, regulatory breaches or student harm.

The University must prohibit retaliation and define how concerns are assessed, investigated and escalated. Confidentiality must be protected as far as possible, subject to fair investigation and law.

# 13. Evidence Management, Records and Audit Trail

## 13.1 Evidence repository

The University must maintain a controlled evidence repository for governance and quality assurance.

Evidence may include:

- Approved statutes, policies and procedures.
- Committee ToR, agendas, minutes and action logs.
- Programme and course proposals and approval records.
- Curriculum maps and learning outcomes.
- Assessment blueprints, rubrics and moderation records.
- Anonymised student work samples where lawful.
- External reviewer reports and institutional responses.
- Staff qualification verification and appointment records.
- Student feedback and analysis.
- Annual and periodic review reports.
- Complaints, appeals and misconduct records under restricted access.
- Risk registers and incident reports.
- Corrective action evidence.
- Regulatory correspondence and licences.
- Accreditation applications and decisions.
- Public catalogue snapshots and claim approvals.

## 13.2 Evidence quality

Evidence must be:

- Authentic and attributable.
- Dated and versioned.
- Relevant to the criterion or decision.
- Complete enough to support the claim.
- Stored with appropriate access controls.
- Retained for the required period.
- Retrievable for audit and external review.

A policy document proves that a policy was written; it does not prove the policy was implemented. Implementation evidence is also required.

## 13.3 Decision records

Every consequential academic decision must record:

- Decision ID.
- Decision type.
- Authority and delegated basis.
- Date and effective date.
- Participants and quorum.
- Conflict declarations and recusals.
- Papers and evidence considered.
- Decision and conditions.
- Rationale.
- Dissent, where recorded under the rules.
- Appeal or review route.
- Follow-up actions.
- Related programme/course/student versions.

## 13.4 Audit trail

The digital platform must preserve an auditable history for:

- Academic policy changes.
- Programme and course versions.
- Approval and publication state changes.
- Assessment configuration and result changes.
- Grade amendments and appeals.
- Student progression and award decisions.
- Staff appointment and permission changes.
- Academic record corrections.
- Access to restricted academic records where appropriate.

Audit events must be append-only or otherwise tamper-evident. Administrators must not be able to erase the history of their own consequential actions.

## 13.5 Record retention

A separate Records Retention and Data Protection Policy must define retention periods based on:

- Applicable law.
- Student protection.
- Qualification and transcript verification.
- Appeals and litigation windows.
- Research requirements.
- Privacy and data minimisation.
- Contractual and regulatory obligations.

This framework does not invent a universal retention period. Records must not be kept forever by default, nor deleted before legitimate obligations expire.

- `EXTERNAL_REVIEWER`

- `STUDENT_SUPPORT`
- `STUDENT`
- `SYSTEM_ADMIN`

Role names are proposed identifiers, not a final permission specification. Permissions must be defined per action and scope, not merely per screen.

## 14.5 Workflow and state enforcement

The system must enforce:

- Draft → submitted → under review → changes requested / recommended → approved / rejected → effective / published.
- Separation between academic approval and technical publication.
- Effective dates and version control.
- Mandatory evidence fields for consequential decisions.
- Conflict declarations and recusal.
- Quorum validation.
- Delegation validity.
- Action ownership and overdue escalation.
- Immutable audit history.
- Restricted access to complaints, appeals, misconduct and personnel files.

## 14.6 Technical security requirements

- Strong authentication for privileged roles.
- Multi-factor authentication for high-impact roles where feasible and legally appropriate.
- Server-side authorisation on every consequential operation.
- No trust in hidden UI buttons as access control.
- Audit logging with protected integrity.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Secure backups and tested restoration.
- Data minimisation and field-level access for sensitive records.
- Secure export and transcript verification.
- Monitoring for unusual administrative activity.
- Incident response and access revocation.
- Separation of production data from development/test data.

## 14.7 Human decision accountability

The platform may route, validate, remind and preserve evidence. It must not autonomously make high-stakes academic decisions such as:

- Conferring a qualification.
- Finding a student guilty of misconduct.
- Determining a complex appeal.
- Approving a new programme.
- Waiving a mandatory academic requirement.
- Appointing an external examiner.
- Declaring an institution accredited.

AI may assist with triage or analysis only under defined policy and human review.

# 15. Academic Quality Indicators and Reporting

## 15.1 KPI principles

Indicators must support judgement, not replace it. They must be defined, contextualised and reviewed for unintended incentives.

No KPI should reward grade inflation, exclusion of weaker students, superficial engagement or avoidance of difficult cases.

## 15.2 Governance and quality KPIs

| KPI | Definition / method | Owner | Review frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme approval completeness | Approved programmes with all mandatory evidence ÷ programmes approved | Quality Office | Quarterly |
| Annual monitoring completion | Reports completed by due date ÷ reports due | AQSC | Annual |
| Periodic review currency | Programmes reviewed within approved cycle ÷ programmes due | AQSC | Quarterly |
| PLO assessment coverage | PLOs with direct assessment evidence ÷ total PLOs | Programme Director | Each course/annual |
| Moderation completion | Required moderation completed ÷ moderation due | Assessment Committee | Each assessment cycle |
| External review action closure | Accepted actions closed and verified by due date ÷ actions due | Quality Office | Quarterly |
| Corrective action ageing | Median days open and count overdue | Action owners / AQSC | Monthly |
| Qualified academic coverage | Academic roles meeting approved qualification/experience criteria ÷ roles requiring them | Academic Affairs | Annual |
| Student complaint resolution | Complaints resolved within published service standard ÷ complaints due | Student Services | Quarterly |
| Appeal process timeliness | Appeals decided within published standard ÷ appeals due | Academic Registry | Quarterly |
| Student feedback action rate | Material feedback themes with documented response/action ÷ themes requiring action | Programme Committees | Annual |
| Academic integrity case consistency | Case outcomes reviewed for policy consistency | AAIC | Annual |
| Evidence retrieval readiness | Sampled evidence records retrievable and complete ÷ records sampled | Quality Office | Semi-annual |
| Governance action completion | Actions completed and verified by due date ÷ actions due | Committee Secretaries | Quarterly |

Each KPI requires a data dictionary specifying numerator, denominator, exclusions, source system, owner, quality checks and limitations.

## 15.3 Context and disaggregation

Where lawful and statistically meaningful, review indicators by:

- Programme and course.
- Cohort and delivery mode.
- Language.
- Assessment type.
- Student entry route.
- Accessibility support needs, with privacy safeguards.
- Relevant demographic categories only where lawful, necessary and proportionate.

Small cohorts must be protected against re-identification.

## 15.4 Reporting calendar

The University should maintain an annual governance calendar covering:

- Academic Council meetings.
- Committee meetings.
- Programme annual monitoring.
- Assessment board windows.
- External review cycles.
- Policy review dates.
- Faculty development reviews.
- Risk register review.
- Board academic assurance reporting.
- Periodic programme review.
- Regulatory reporting deadlines.

# 16. Conflicts of Interest, Confidentiality and Ethical Conduct

## 16.1 Conflict of interest

A conflict may be actual, potential or perceived. It may arise from financial, personal, professional, family, supervisory, authorship or commercial relationships.

Members and staff must:

- Declare relevant interests at appointment and when circumstances change.
- Declare agenda-specific conflicts before discussion.
- Follow recusal instructions.
- Avoid access to restricted material where conflicted.
- Not influence decisions through informal channels.
- Record management of the conflict.

A register of interests must be maintained with access proportionate to confidentiality and law.

## 16.2 Confidentiality

Confidentiality applies to:

- Student records and assessment submissions.
- Complaints, appeals and misconduct cases.
- Staff qualifications, contracts and performance.
- Commercially sensitive papers.
- Security incidents.
- Research participant data.
- External reviewer confidential material.

Confidentiality must not be used to conceal wrongdoing or prevent lawful reporting.

## 16.3 Ethical conduct

Governance members and staff must act with honesty, fairness, independence, competence, respect and accountability. The institution must maintain a code of conduct and a route for reporting suspected breaches.

# 22. Institutional Rule

Academic governance is not a collection of titles, committees or approval buttons. It is a functioning system of independent judgement, explicit authority, qualified people, transparent rules, reliable evidence, student protection and corrective action.

Every consequential academic decision must be able to answer:

- Who had authority to make it?
- Under which approved rule or delegation?
- Who participated, and was quorum met?
- Were conflicts declared and managed?
- What evidence was considered?
- What was decided, and why?
- Which students, programmes, courses or versions are affected?
- How can the decision be reviewed or appealed?
- Where is the evidence retained?
- How will the institution verify that the decision worked?

**The University must not merely document quality. It must operate quality, measure it, challenge it and prove it.**
